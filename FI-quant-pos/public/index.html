Step 1 — Set regime first, every morning
Check Nifty vs its 200-day moving average on your broker chart. Check India VIX. Set regime in the Today tab header or Trade → Alerts. This changes all signal weights — getting this wrong biases every score.
Nifty > 200DMA + VIX < 14 = Bull Nifty ≈ 200DMA + VIX 14-18 = Range Nifty < 200DMA or VIX > 18 = Bear
Step 2 — Read a trade card correctly
Every alert card shows Bayesian score (the probability), direction, signal tags, and when expanded: entry zone, hard SL, T1 (book 50%), T2 (trail rest), Kelly-sized position, and the 4-step trailing SL protocol.
Score ≥72% green = full Kelly size Score 62-72% blue = half size Score 50-62% amber = watchlist only Score < 62% = do not trade
Step 3 — The 4-step trailing SL protocol
This is the most important rule in the system. Never deviate from it.
① Enter: Hard SL placed. No manual override. ② T1 hit: Book 50%. Move SL → entry price. Trade is now RISK-FREE. ③ Price near T2: Trail SL up to T1 level. ④ T2 hit: Exit remaining 50% or hold with 1% trailing stop.
Step 4 — Journal every trade
After placing any trade, add it to the Paper Trade journal (Track → Paper Trade → Add trade). When it closes, mark it Win or Loss. After 20 closed trades, your expectancy number becomes meaningful. Before that — you have no edge data.
17 signals across 6 families
Price / Momentum (4 signals)
RSI breakout >50 · EMA 20/50 crossover · Supertrend flip · ADX >25

Best in: Bull regime. Win rate 53–58% in bull, falls to 36–40% in bear. These are your primary engine when markets are trending.
Volume (3 signals)
Volume surge >2× average · OBV trend break · Delivery % spike

Confirms whether a price move has institutional conviction. Volume surge on a breakout is much stronger than breakout alone. Delivery % spike = genuine buying, not intraday noise.
Mean Reversion (3 signals)
Bollinger Band touch · Z-score >2.0 deviation · VWAP deviation

Best in: Range regime. Win rate 54–57% in range. These fire when a stock is statistically extended from its mean — either oversold bounce or overbought short.
Options flow (3 signals)
PCR extreme (<0.7 or >1.3) · OI buildup at strikes · IV rank <20

Institutional positioning in options telegraphs directional bias before the cash market moves. PCR <0.7 = too many calls = complacency = bearish. PCR >1.3 = too much fear = contrarian bullish.
Macro (2 signals)
FII net buying streak · India VIX <14

These act as multipliers, not trade signals. FII buying increases win rate of momentum signals by ~5–8%. High VIX suppresses all signal weights — the system becomes more conservative automatically.
Alt data (2 signals)
Insider / bulk deal buying · Block deal prints

Highest per-signal win rate: 58–62%. Infrequent but powerful. When an insider buys stock in their own company on BSE bulk deals data, the Bayesian score jumps more than any technical signal.
The math — Bayesian + Kelly explained simply
// Bayesian update — how P(win) is computed Start: P(win) = 45% ← NSE base rate Each signal updates it: P(win | signal fires) = P(signal|win) × P(win) ───────────────────────── P(signal fires) Example: RSI breakout fires in bull market → Likelihood ratio = 1.45 (bull regime) → P updates from 45% → 54% Volume surge also fires: → LR = 1.55 (bull regime) → P updates from 54% → 63% FII buying also fires: → LR = 1.40 → P updates from 63% → 71% Score = 71% → TAKE THE TRADE (≥62%)
// Kelly Criterion — position sizing f* = (p × b − q) / b Where: p = P(win) from Bayesian e.g. 0.71 q = 1 − p e.g. 0.29 b = reward:risk ratio e.g. 2.0x Example at 71% win, 2× RR: f* = (0.71 × 2 − 0.29) / 2 = (1.42 − 0.29) / 2 = 0.565 → 56.5% of capital But full Kelly is too aggressive. Capped at 25% of corpus always. At ₹10L corpus: 25% = ₹2.5L per trade This is the hard maximum position size.
💡 Key insight: two signals scoring 55% each give a combined score higher than either alone — because Bayesian updating is multiplicative, not additive. Confluence of signals is mathematically powerful.
⚠ The scores are probabilistic estimates based on historical win rates, not guarantees. A 72% score means you should win roughly 7 out of 10 times — not that this specific trade will win. Never risk more than 1.5% of capital per trade regardless of score.
Auto-Lab — how the strategy evolution engine works
What it does
Auto-Lab continuously runs paper trades across all strategies simultaneously during market hours (9:15–15:10 IST, Mon–Fri only). Each trade is assigned to a strategy. Results feed back into strategy stats in real time.
Promotion rules
After 10+ trades, a strategy is evaluated. Promoted to "FI Candidate" status if win rate ≥75% AND avg RR ≥2.5×. These thresholds are configurable in Settings → Auto-Lab settings.
Win rate ≥75% + RR ≥2.5 = PROMOTED ✓ 5 consecutive losses = RETIRED ✗ 15 trades + WR < 40% = RETIRED ✗
Strategy mutation
Every 8 iterations, the engine generates a variant of an existing strategy by swapping one signal. This is genetic algorithm logic — the strongest strategies spawn variants, which compete and either get promoted or retired.
Auto-Journal
Every trade — win or loss — is logged automatically with entry reason, exit reason, market context, and an AI-generated lesson. After 50+ trades, the Key Learnings panel surfaces patterns across all losses and wins.
How to use it
Start Auto-Lab once at 9:15 AM. Let it run all day. Stop it at 3:30 PM (or let auto sq-off handle it). Review the Strategies page weekly — promoted strategies with real edge become your live trading playbook.
Speed setting
The cycle interval (default 2000ms) controls how fast paper trades fire and close. In backtest mode (after hours), you can set it to 500ms to run through weeks of simulation quickly. During market hours, 2000ms is realistic.
Risk rules — these are non-negotiable
89% of F&O traders lose money (SEBI data, FY22). 40 lakh individuals lost avg ₹1.1L each. The difference between you and them is these six rules — executed without exception, every single day.
Rule 1 — 1.5% risk per trade max
Never risk more than 1.5% of total corpus on any single trade. On ₹10L, that is ₹15,000 max loss. Kelly criterion enforces this automatically — the position size output already respects this limit.
Rule 2 — Daily loss circuit breaker
If today's realised loss exceeds your daily loss limit (default 2% of corpus = ₹20,000 on ₹10L), the app shows a red banner and disables Auto-Lab and scanning. Stop trading. Review tomorrow.
Rule 3 — Stop loss is sacred
Never move a stop loss further away from entry to avoid a loss. Never average down on a losing position. Once a stop is set, it stays there or moves only in the profitable direction (trailing SL).
Rule 4 — 3 consecutive losses = pause
Three consecutive losses means the market regime may have changed, or your signals are not working. Stop trading. Review the last 3 trades in the journal. Re-assess regime. Resume only after an honest review.
Rule 5 — Max 3 open positions
Never have more than 3 positions open simultaneously in manual trading. More than 3 and you cannot manage them properly. Position correlation also increases — 3 LONG trades in the same sector is not 3 positions, it is 1 concentrated bet.
Rule 6 — Do not trade on earnings week
Exit any momentum trades 2 days before a company's earnings announcement. Gap risk on results day can breach your stop loss instantly with no exit opportunity. The app does not yet flag earnings dates — check NSE calendar manually.

Frequently asked questions
Why is my Bayesian score different every time I scan?+
The scanner uses simulated price data (until you connect the Vercel + Yahoo Finance live data layer). Each scan generates fresh OHLCV-based signals. Once you deploy to Vercel and enable live data in Settings, scores reflect real NSE prices with 15-minute delay.
When should I switch from paper to live trading?+
The Paper Trade tab shows a readiness score. The minimum requirements: 40 closed paper trades, win rate ≥55%, Sharpe ≥1.2, and positive expectancy. Do not rush this — a single bad live trade can wipe what took months of paper trading to build psychologically.
Why does Auto-Lab only run during market hours?+
Paper trades simulating NSE stocks must use market-hours timestamps to be meaningful. Trades "taken" at 11 PM on a Sunday don't reflect real market conditions. In backtest mode (after hours), you can still run the backtest engine on synthetic historical data.
What is the realistic return expectation?+
A well-designed systematic strategy on Indian equities should target 15–25% CAGR with Sharpe above 1.0 and max drawdown under 20%. The FI calculator defaults to 20% for this reason. Anyone claiming consistent 35%+ without leverage is either backtesting on a lucky period, using leverage, or lying.
How do I deploy this app to Vercel for live data?+
Go to Settings → Deploy to Vercel section for the full 5-step instructions. Short version: push index.html + api/quote.js + api/nse.js + vercel.json to a GitHub repo → connect to Vercel → deploy. Your live URL appears in 30 seconds. Then set the API base URL in Settings and switch live data mode to "Yahoo Finance (Vercel)".
Does this app place real trades automatically?+
No. This version is a decision-support and simulation tool. Auto-Lab runs paper trades only. All real trades are placed manually in your broker (Shoonya or any other). The Shoonya API integration for automated order placement is planned for a future version.
What does "regime" mean and why does it matter so much?+
Regime is the market's current character: Bull (trending up), Range (sideways), or Bear (trending down). Each regime favours different signal families — momentum works in bull, mean reversion works in range, options signals dominate in bear. Setting the wrong regime biases every score in the app. Check Nifty vs 200DMA + VIX each morning.
Why is Kelly capped at 25%?+
Full Kelly maximises long-term geometric growth in theory, but assumes perfectly calibrated probability estimates. In practice, our estimates are approximate — and full Kelly on an over-estimated edge leads to catastrophic drawdowns. Half-Kelly (12.5%) to Quarter-Kelly (6.25%) is what most professional quants use. The 25% cap is already conservative — many practitioners cap at 10–15%.
